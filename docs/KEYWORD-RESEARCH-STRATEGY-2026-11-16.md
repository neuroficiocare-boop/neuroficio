# NeuroFicio keyword research and editorial strategy

Researched and editorial batch prepared 16 November 2026. The owner authorised publishing without an Ahrefs connection. Search-volume ranking remains unverified; publication proceeds on service relevance.

## Recommendation

Target the highest measured search demand within relevant rehabilitation topics, while keeping a useful route from each guide to an actual NeuroFicio service. Build fewer, more complete guides around distinct patient questions rather than a separate page for every keyword variation. Update existing coverage where the search intent is already addressed.

## What is verified and what is not

- The current site has 20 published guides and six service categories. Existing pages and source content were inspected.
- Public search-result checks were performed for Hyderabad home neurological care, overseas families arranging rehabilitation in India, international home programmes, virtual neurorehabilitation, speech/swallowing and rehabilitation technology. Results show relevant service-page patterns, but do not establish search volume, rank difficulty or patient demand.
- The following list is a service-filtered candidate keyword universe. Monthly search volumes and organic difficulty have NOT been measured. Do not call these proven high-volume keywords or attach invented numbers.
- Ahrefs was found as an available integration but is not confirmed installed/connected. A suggestion was provided. Google Keyword Planner exports are an alternative. Actual data access is needed before a defensible high-volume ranking can be produced.
- A search-result snippet is not evidence that its medical claims are correct. Competitor material was used only to inspect search intent; it is not a clinical source and will not be copied.

## High-volume selection method

1. Exclude a query unless its intent fits an actual service, patient group and geography. Adult neurological rehabilitation is the current core scope; avoid broad unrelated musculoskeletal traffic.
2. Obtain country-specific monthly search-volume estimates, organic ranking difficulty, trend and search-result intent from the connected provider. Use global volume only as an additional reach signal.
3. Separate patient location from enquirer location. Overseas families may search from countries outside the 18-country delivery list while arranging care for someone in Hyderabad. That does not establish in-person delivery in the enquirer's country.
4. Rank the remaining topics by measured demand, clinical/service relevance, likely enquiry intent, attainable search-result format and existing coverage. A large broad keyword should support a helpful hub; related decision queries can be addressed within the same guide.
5. Keep one main destination per search intent. Service pages answer booking/availability questions; blog guides answer educational and decision questions. Link them together instead of making them compete with nearly identical content.
6. Treat advertising competition in Keyword Planner separately from organic SEO difficulty. Treat Search Console impressions separately from total market search volume. Preserve source, country, language, date range and provider notes alongside any metric.
7. Do not add close-variant volumes together blindly: providers may group variants or report overlapping demand. No arbitrary search-volume minimum has been selected before looking at each country and niche.

## Geographic relevance

The 18 owner-supplied countries are Tanzania, Kenya, United Arab Emirates, Oman, Maldives, Qatar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, New Zealand, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Fiji, Trinidad and Tobago, Mongolia, Bhutan and Laos. Keep the shared country list already used on the website as the authority for enquiry geography.

Country-modified phrases such as international home rehabilitation in [country], virtual stroke rehabilitation in [country], and rehabilitation equipment sourcing in [country] are candidates for measurement, not instructions to generate 54 near-identical articles. Only create a dedicated country guide when verified local service arrangements, professional requirements and practical details make it independently useful. Do not invent clinics, local staff, addresses, immediate deployment or local registrations.

For India, evaluate Hyderabad and the existing listed home-visit areas. Do not automatically extrapolate confirmed Hyderabad arrangements into nationwide immediate home visits. For Trinidad and Tobago, preserve the existing Port of Spain / San Fernando clarification.

## Clinical accuracy workflow

- Build a claim-to-source record for each article before drafting. Prefer current clinical guidelines, systematic reviews and appropriate primary studies; do not use supplier marketing as evidence of benefit.
- Relevant starting resources include WHO's neurological rehabilitation intervention package, NICE NG236 for adult stroke, and NICE NG252 for chronic neurological disorders/acquired injury. Check the exact population, intervention, setting and exclusions rather than applying a recommendation to all neurological conditions.
- NICE NG252 was published in October 2025 and should be considered when updating older acquired-injury/chronic-neurological content. It does not replace the adult stroke guideline.
- Technology claims require indication-specific scrutiny. For example, NICE NG236 advises against robotic arm training as part of post-stroke upper-limb rehabilitation. This is not a blanket statement about all robotics, but it prevents describing every advanced device as universally recommended or superior. Electrical stimulation and EMG biofeedback also need population- and intervention-specific evidence.
- Some NICE full-page fetches were blocked with HTTP 403 during this research. Official indexed recommendation extracts were available. Obtain and review the full relevant recommendation/evidence document before using it to author a clinical article; this strategy document is not a completed clinical evidence review.
- Explain uncertainty, limitations and differences between guidance systems. Do not promise recovery, a fixed timeline, guaranteed walking, superiority of a device, or a session frequency that is not individually assessed.
- Do not provide unassessed swallowing manoeuvres, food/fluid changes, stimulation settings, medication advice or transfer protocols as universal instructions.
- Treatment-related guides should be signed off by an appropriately qualified clinician for that discipline. Speech/swallowing material needs the relevant specialist. Publish real reviewer names, credentials and review dates only after review occurs. The owner's verified team details are still pending.
- Use actual business facts for staff, treatment access, fees, country requirements and equipment availability. Citations to medical literature do not verify NeuroFicio's operational claims.

## Enquiry design

Every relevant guide should provide a clear enquiry path, but repeated sales banners should not overwhelm the answer. Use a short contextual enquiry banner after the introduction or a relevant decision section, a desktop sidebar / unobtrusive mobile contact action, and the existing end-of-guide enquiry form. The current 20 articles already include contextual forms, so improve their placement and relevance instead of adding duplicative popups.

Link each guide to the correct service, include a shareable brochure where useful, and retain the separate patient-country/enquirer-country fields and privacy acknowledgement. A WhatsApp handoff is a click, not a received assessment request or booked patient. Emergency-intent pages must prioritise urgent local care over routine enquiries. Do not send names, diagnoses, phone numbers, records or free-text enquiries into analytics.

## Measurement and publication sequence

1. Complete measured keyword research and approve the initial topic order. Review existing Google Search Console and Bing query/page/country data when available.
2. Audit the 20 existing guides and agree merges, updates or retirements without silently deleting traffic-bearing URLs.
3. Draft the provisional 12-topic first batch, updating existing articles where appropriate. A smaller release is acceptable if evidence or verified service information is incomplete.
4. Complete clinical and business-fact review; add only truthful reviewer/author metadata, dates and structured data that matches visible content.
5. Publish the reviewed batch, check internal links/canonicals/sitemap and verify the live pages. Request discovery/index inspection through the appropriate webmaster tools when available.
6. Monitor impressions, clicks, country, query, landing page and consented contact actions, then qualified enquiries, assessments booked and programmes started through an actual agreed lead-tracking process. No CRM connection or booking success event is currently implemented.
7. Expand the next distinct topics based on actual impressions and qualified enquiry evidence. Search volume is an estimate, not a traffic or patient guarantee.
